About

Electronics has held my attention since I was a teenager — long before it was a career, it was what I did with my free time, teaching myself the fundamentals and building circuits on my own. That early curiosity turned into an Associate's degree, a Bachelor's in Electronics Engineering, and an M.Sc. in Digital Electronic Systems at Shahid Beheshti University, where each step pulled me toward a broader view of how electronics underpins nearly every industry it touches.

I'm now a Ph.D. student and research assistant in the Department of Automotive Engineering at Clemson University. My research background spans neuromorphic computing, energy-efficient devices, and neural-inspired AI acceleration — work that taught me to care as much about noise robustness and device variation as about accuracy on paper. That grounding now feeds into my current focus: power electronics and power system controls, where I work on improving system performance, efficiency, and reliability, alongside hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) validation and machine learning.
